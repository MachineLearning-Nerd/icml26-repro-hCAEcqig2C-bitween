\[
f(x+r)=\sin\!\big(e^{x+r}\big)=\sin\!\big(e^{r}\,e^{x}\big).
\]

If the multiplier \(e^{r}\) happens to be an **integer** \(n\ge 1\) then we are
evaluating \(\sin(n\,e^{x})\).  The sine of an integer multiple of a variable can
be written as a polynomial in \(\sin(\cdot)\) using the standard multiple‑angle
formulas (which are independent of any trigonometric sign choices when we work
with squares).  Hence, for any integer \(n\),

\[
\sin\big(n\,e^{x}\big)=P_{n}\!\big(\sin(e^{x})\big)=P_{n}\!\big(f(x)\big),
\]

where \(P_{n}\) is the well‑known Chebyshev‑type polynomial for the sine
multiple‑angle identity.  Choosing \(r=\ln n\) therefore gives a **randomized
self‑reduction**:

\[
f\big(x+\ln n\big)=P_{n}\!\big(f(x)\big).
\]
